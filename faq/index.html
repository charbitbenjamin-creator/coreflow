FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Direct answers about what CoreFlow does, what is in our scope, and how a liquid cooling loop is designed, installed, commissioned and maintained.

About CoreFlow

What does CoreFlow do?

CoreFlow delivers liquid cooling for European data centers from design to maintenance: the full loop between the facility water and the racks, CDUs included, with a single partner accountable end to end. We design or review the loop in 3D BIM, supply and install the CDUs, pre-assembled manifolds, pipework and insulation, then flush, pressure-test and commission it with a full quality dossier, and monitor and maintain it for the life of the system.

Is CoreFlow a cooling equipment manufacturer?

No. CoreFlow is a service team, not a product vendor, and is independent of cooling manufacturers. We supply and install CDUs and components as part of a project, selected for that project.

Who does CoreFlow work for?

AI clouds and enterprise data centers, directly. And cooling manufacturers, as their local second line for maintenance.

Where does CoreFlow operate?

CoreFlow is based in Paris, France, and works on data center projects across Europe, with the same written procedures from one site to the next.

Who founded CoreFlow?

CoreFlow was founded in 2026 by Benjamin Charbit, who leads the company as Founder and CEO. Its first projects are in preparation.

Scope

Which part of the cooling system does CoreFlow cover?

The full loop between the facility water and the racks, CDUs included: CDUs in-row or at the demarcation point, stainless pipework, insulation, pre-assembled manifolds, sensors and the connection at each rack. See the scope element by element.

Does CoreFlow take on the primary loop?

Yes, as an option. We can take on the primary loop, on the facility water side, so that the client deals with a single counterpart.

Does CoreFlow supply the CDUs?

Yes. We select the CDUs and their redundancy with the client, then supply, install and commission them.

Does CoreFlow work on servers and cold plates?

No. Servers and cold plates stay with the hardware manufacturer or the integrator. We connect the loop at each rack and commission it to the hardware manufacturer's requirements on cleanliness and fluid chemistry.

Working with CoreFlow

When should we bring CoreFlow in?

As early as possible. We plan backwards from the hardware delivery date, so that the loop is ready when the racks arrive. Design is often on the critical path of a liquid-cooled deployment.

Can CoreFlow work from an existing design?

Yes. When the design is already fixed, we step in on execution alone, from installation to commissioning and handover, after a review of the design.

Can CoreFlow work in a data center that stays in operation?

Yes. Extensions and air-to-liquid conversions are done on live sites, with a method of procedure for each intervention, integrated in the operator's change management, and with leak risk controlled by drip trays, zoned detection and isolation rack by rack.

What does CoreFlow need to prepare a quote?

The number of racks and the power per rack, the temperatures and delta T of the facility and of the hardware manufacturer, the CDU configuration and redundancy, the room drawings and distances, the access rules, and the specified fluid with the manufacturer's requirements. The full list is on the contact page.

Commissioning and quality

How is a liquid cooling loop commissioned?

The loop is proven leak-tight by a recorded pressure test, then chemically cleaned, flushed section by section through filters from 100 to 50 to 5 µm, disinfected, filled with premixed coolant, vented and degassed. A laboratory analysis is done before the racks are connected. Acceptance is on a pass or fail basis. See commissioning in detail.

What does CoreFlow commit to?

The leak-tightness of the loop, proven by a recorded pressure test. Conformity to the approved design, joint by joint. The workmanship of pipework and valves, with every joint traced. Instrumentation and acceptance tests on a pass or fail basis. And water quality at handover, backed by a dated analysis.

What do we receive at handover?

A full quality dossier: joint-by-joint traceability, test reports, a dated water analysis, as-built drawings and the operating procedures.

Maintenance

What does maintenance include?

3 building blocks that can be combined: preventive maintenance of the loop, water analysis, and on-call support with a response time fixed in the contract. Temperature, pressure, flow and leaks are monitored and reported to the client's BMS.

Can CoreFlow maintain equipment on behalf of a cooling manufacturer?

Yes. CoreFlow can act as the local second line of a cooling manufacturer for the maintenance of its installed equipment in Europe.
