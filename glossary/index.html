Liquid cooling glossary

As-built drawings
Drawings updated at the end of a project to show the installation as it was really built, including every change made on site. They are part of the handover dossier and are the reference for operation and maintenance.
BIM Building information modelling
A 3D model of a building and its equipment in which every object carries data, such as its diameter, material or reference. In liquid cooling, the loop is designed in 3D BIM inside the real room, so that clashes with racks, floors and existing equipment are found before installation.
Biocide
A chemical that kills bacteria and other micro-organisms. A loop is disinfected with a biocide during commissioning, with a controlled contact time, because warm water favours bacterial growth that can clog filters and cold plates.
BMS Building management system
The supervision system of a building, which collects the measurements of its technical equipment and raises alarms. The temperature, pressure, flow and leak sensors of a liquid cooling loop report to the BMS, sometimes alongside a DCIM, the tool that supervises the IT rooms.
CDU Coolant distribution unit
The unit that separates the facility water from the loop that serves the IT equipment, and transfers heat from one to the other. A CDU contains a heat exchanger, pumps, filtration and controls. It regulates the flow, temperature and pressure of the coolant sent to the racks. See also in-row and in-rack CDU.
Cold plate
A metal plate with very fine internal channels, mounted directly on a processor or another hot component. The coolant flows through the plate and carries the heat away. Because the channels are so fine, cold plates are sensitive to particles and to the chemistry of the fluid.
Commissioning
The set of checks and tests that prove an installation works as designed before it enters service. In data centers it is usually organised in 5 levels, from L1 (factory tests) to L5 (integrated systems test). For a liquid cooling loop it includes the pressure test, cleaning, flushing, disinfection, filling and analysis of the fluid. See how CoreFlow commissions a loop.
Corrosion inhibitor
An additive that protects the metals of the loop against corrosion. It is consumed over time, which is why its concentration is measured regularly during maintenance.
Delta T ΔT
The difference between the return temperature and the supply temperature of the coolant. Together with the flow rate, it determines how much heat the loop removes. The facility and the hardware manufacturer each set their own temperature limits, and the design has to satisfy both.
Demarcation point
The agreed physical boundary between what the data center operator provides and what the tenant or the IT side is responsible for. In liquid cooling it is often a CDU or a pair of valved connections on the facility water.
Direct liquid cooling DLC, direct-to-chip
A cooling method in which a liquid is brought to cold plates mounted on the hottest components of a server, such as GPUs and CPUs. The liquid removes most of the heat, and air removes the rest. It is the method used for the latest generations of AI racks, which draw more than 100 kW each.
Facility water system FWS
The water loop of the building, which brings cooling water from the plant (chillers, dry coolers or cooling towers) to the CDUs. The term comes from the ASHRAE liquid cooling guidelines.
Flushing
The circulation of water through a new loop, at a flow above the nominal rate and through progressively finer filters, to remove the particles, grease and residues left by installation. Flushing is done before the racks are connected.
Handover dossier
The set of documents given to the operator at the end of a project. At CoreFlow it contains joint-by-joint traceability, the test reports, a dated water analysis, the as-built drawings and the operating procedures.
In-row and in-rack CDU
An in-row CDU is a cabinet placed in the row of racks, which serves several racks through pipework and manifolds. An in-rack CDU is installed inside a rack and serves that rack. Larger CDUs can also be placed at the edge of the room, at the demarcation point.
Insulation
The layer that covers the pipework to prevent condensation on cold surfaces and to limit heat exchange with the room.
Leak detection
Sensing cables or point sensors placed under the pipework, in drip trays and at the connections, which raise an alarm when they detect liquid. Detection is zoned so that a leak can be located and the affected section isolated.
Manifold
A component that distributes the coolant from the pipework to several outlets and collects the return. Row manifolds serve the racks, and rack manifolds serve the servers. Manifolds can be pre-assembled before they enter the data hall.
MOP, SOP, EOP
The 3 types of written procedures used in data centers. A method of procedure (MOP) describes a specific intervention step by step, and is approved through the operator's change management. A standard operating procedure (SOP) describes routine operation. An emergency operating procedure (EOP) describes what to do in an incident, such as a leak.
P&ID Piping and instrumentation diagram
The diagram that shows every pipe, valve, pump and sensor of a loop and how they are connected. It is the reference document of the design.
PG25
A water-based coolant that contains 25 % propylene glycol, with corrosion inhibitors and biocides. It is commonly specified by hardware manufacturers for direct liquid cooling loops, and is delivered premixed.
Pressure test
A test in which the loop is pressurised above its operating pressure and held for a set time, while the pressure is recorded. A stable pressure proves that the loop is leak-tight before it is filled with coolant.
Primary and secondary loop
In liquid cooling, the primary loop usually means the facility water side of the CDU, and the secondary loop the side that serves the racks. The meaning of these words varies from one company to another, so it is safer to describe the physical loop: from the facility water to the racks.
Quick disconnect
A coupling that connects a server or a rack to a manifold and can be disconnected without draining the loop and with almost no spill. Its small internal passages make it sensitive to particles.
Technology cooling system TCS
In the ASHRAE liquid cooling guidelines, the loop that carries the treated coolant between the CDU and the IT equipment. Depending on the position of the CDU, it runs across the room or stays inside a rack.
Water quality
The set of parameters the coolant has to keep within limits: particles, pH, conductivity, glycol and inhibitor concentration, and bacterial count. The limits are set by the hardware manufacturer. Water quality is proven by a laboratory analysis at handover, then followed during maintenance.
